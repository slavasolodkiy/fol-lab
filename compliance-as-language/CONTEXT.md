# Research context: from evidence gates to compliance as a language

## The question in this sprint

Can a useful, bounded part of identity and financial-crime compliance be expressed as reusable, inspectable rules—while keeping uncertainty, institutional policy, and human judgement visible? The larger hypothesis is **compliance as a language**: shared concepts, predicates, evidence and decision rules that can be discussed and reused. This prototype tests a small part of that hypothesis. It does not show that all compliance can be reduced to formulas, that source facts are true, or that an institution is legally compliant.

The author’s experience of KYC, KYB, customer-of-customer risk, ongoing monitoring, enhanced due diligence and correspondent banking motivates the question. The narrow illustrative implementation covers person onboarding, company onboarding, and transaction evidence; it does not purport to implement all those activities. The fictional predicates and policy choices are not statements of law.

## What this prototype actually does

The current Python evaluator takes a fictional case type, a subject, an evaluation time, and supplied evidence items. It checks the shape of each record, requires the evidence subject to match the case subject, and excludes evidence that is not yet observed or has expired. For each required predicate, current evidence is classified as `true`, `false`, `unknown`, or `conflict`. Unknown and conflict do not become a clear result simply because no adverse fact is present.

Under this **illustrative policy**, `screening_clear = false` returns `review`, not `deny`. A current, uncontradicted assertion `policy_hard_stop = true` returns `deny`; `permit` requires all other required facts to be supported and an explicit, uncontradicted `policy_hard_stop = false`. Incomplete or conflicting evidence returns `review`, except where a separately asserted uncontradicted hard stop has priority. A decision is a result under fictional inputs and fictional rules, not a finding that a person is sanctioned, a judgement of criminality, a regulatory conclusion, or an instruction to execute a real transaction. Evidence sources are labels supplied by the caller, not independently validated attestations. Time bounds alone do not establish reliability or revocation status. The code and tests are the implementation record.
